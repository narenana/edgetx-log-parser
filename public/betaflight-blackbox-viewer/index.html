Betaflight blackbox log viewer for crash review and GPS replay

RC Log Viewer is a Betaflight log viewer that runs in your browser. Drop a .bbl, .bfl or .txt blackbox log onto the tool and it decodes it on your machine, then draws synced charts for attitude (roll, pitch and yaw), altitude, battery and radio signal, with your stick inputs in the telemetry bar. If the quad carries a GPS, you also get a 3D replay of the flight on a satellite globe. Nothing installs and nothing uploads; the log is parsed locally with WebAssembly. It reads logs from Betaflight 4.5.x and earlier.

Use it to see where a GPS quad went and to work out what happened in a crash. Most racing and freestyle quads log no GPS, so the 3D globe stays off, but the charts and telemetry bar work on every log. For gyro, PID and filter tuning, use Betaflight's own Blackbox Viewer; the section below explains the difference.

What you get from a Betaflight log (GPS or not)

Betaflight blackbox is mostly about what the flight controller was doing, not where it was. RC Log Viewer reads the record and gives you:

  • Stick inputs in the telemetry bar: your stick positions at the playhead, so you can line up a twitch or a crash with what you actually asked the quad to do.
  • Attitude: roll, pitch and yaw, so you can see oscillation, bounce-back or a flip.
  • Battery: pack voltage and per-cell voltage in the live telemetry bar, so you can spot a sag that browned out the FC mid-punch.
  • Altitude & vertical speed and signal, where the log includes them.

Scrub the timeline and every chart plus the telemetry bar move together, so you read one moment across all channels at once. It does not draw gyro, PID, motor or spectrum traces. For a walkthrough of what each trace means, see how to read a blackbox log.

Why most Betaflight logs skip the 3D globe

The 3D satellite replay needs real GPS coordinates in the log. Racing and freestyle quads usually fly without a GPS module, so a Betaflight blackbox from a typical quad has no position data — the globe is simply unavailable for that log. That's expected, not a failure of the file.

Everything else still works. The telemetry bar and all synced charts read fine from a GPS-less log, which is what you need for crash review. If your quad does carry a GPS (a long-range or cruiser build), those coordinates get picked up and the globe becomes available. GPS-equipped and fixed-wing builds on iNAV are more likely to have full position — that's covered on the iNAV log viewer page.

Opening a .bbl, .bfl, or .txt file

Betaflight writes blackbox in a few shapes depending on where it was logged and how you pulled it off:

  • .bbl / .bfl — the raw binary blackbox, either from onboard dataflash or an SD card.
  • .txt — a log some setups export; RC Log Viewer reads these too.

Steps: open the viewer, then drag the file in (or click to browse). One thing to know about the format: a single .bbl can contain more than one arm/disarm session concatenated into the same file, so a big file is often several short flights back to back. The viewer opens the first session in the file. If you're not sure which format you have or how to get the file off the quad, see how to open a .bbl file.

How this differs from Betaflight's own Blackbox Viewer

Betaflight's own Blackbox Viewer is built into the Betaflight App at app.betaflight.com, and the standalone version is still online at blackbox.betaflight.com. Both run in the browser. It is the tool for gyro, PID and filter tuning: it draws gyro, PID and motor traces and has a spectrum analyser. This viewer draws none of those.

Use this one when you want to see where a GPS quad went, and what the sticks and battery were doing when it crashed:

  • 3D GPS replay on a satellite globe, with chase, tail, orbit and top-down cameras, when the quad carries a GPS.
  • Crash review: attitude, altitude, battery and signal charts on one timeline, with your stick inputs in the telemetry bar.
  • Nothing uploaded: parsing happens on your machine with WebAssembly. When a GPS is fitted, your blackbox embeds the coordinates of your flying field, and they stay on your machine.
  • iNAV and EdgeTX logs too: the same viewer opens iNAV blackbox logs and EdgeTX radio CSV logs.

There's also an installable PWA that works offline, plus free desktop builds for Windows, macOS and Linux on GitHub releases.

Reviewing a crash

For a suspected crash or desync, work backwards from the end of the log. Scrub to the last moments and read the channels together: did battery voltage sag hard right before things went wrong? Did the stick inputs in the telemetry bar show a command the quad didn't follow (a sign of failsafe or a motor giving up)? Did attitude diverge from your stick input? Because the charts and telemetry bar are synced to one timeline, you can pin the exact instant and see every channel at once. There's a full method on analysing a drone crash log.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Betaflight blackbox viewer need GPS in the log?
No. GPS is only needed for the optional 3D globe replay. Most racing and freestyle Betaflight logs have no GPS, and the telemetry bar and every synced chart (attitude, altitude, battery, signal) still work.
What file formats does it accept?
Betaflight blackbox as .bbl, .bfl, or .txt. iNAV blackbox and EdgeTX/OpenTX CSV telemetry logs are also supported by the same viewer.
Which Betaflight versions are supported?
Betaflight blackbox up to 4.5.x. Logs from Betaflight 2025.12 and later are not tested yet and may not open. Logs are decoded in your browser with WebAssembly.
Is my log uploaded anywhere?
No. Parsing is 100% client-side, so the log never leaves your machine. That matters because your blackbox can embed the GPS coordinates of your flying field when a GPS module is fitted.
How is this different from Betaflight's Blackbox Viewer?
Betaflight's own Blackbox Viewer, in the Betaflight App at app.betaflight.com, is the tool for gyro, PID and filter tuning. This viewer doesn't draw gyro, PID or motor traces. Use this one to see where a GPS quad went and what the sticks, battery and signal were doing before a crash.
My .bbl seems to hold several flights. Can it read that?
It opens the first one. A single .bbl often holds several arm/disarm sessions back to back, and the viewer currently decodes only the first session in the file.
